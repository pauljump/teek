# At-a-Distance Psychological Assessment: A Methods Dossier for Implementation

> **Provenance.** Produced 2026-08-04 by a Claude Sonnet research agent with web access, commissioned for the Teek v3 rebuild. This is the source document behind `docs/method.md`. Paul Jump has no training in psychological assessment; this dossier is the AI-acquired expertise that the method is built on, and that dependency is disclosed rather than obscured. Claims here carry the reliability of the agent's sourcing, which is cited inline throughout.

This dossier covers the operational-code tradition, trait-and-motive coding schemes, integrative complexity, computational personality-from-text, values/moral frameworks, personal construct elicitation, and adjacent methods (cognitive mapping, image theory, narrative identity), followed by a dedicated validity/failure-mode section and a build recommendation.

## 1. Operational Code Analysis (Leites → George → Walker/Schafer/Young VICS)

**What it measures.** A leader's belief system about the nature of politics and the rules for acting in it, organized as answers to Alexander George's ten questions, split into five *philosophical* beliefs (nature of political conflict, prospects for realizing values, predictability of the political universe, control over historical development, role of chance) and five *instrumental* beliefs (best approach to selecting goals, pursuing goals, calculating/controlling/accepting risk, timing of action, utility of different means, e.g., cooperation vs. conflict). George reformulated Nathan Leites's dense Bolshevik-elite study into this ten-question checklist as a general diagnostic frame for any political actor. ([RAND](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_memoranda/RM5427.html), [openeclass PDF](https://openeclass.uom.gr/modules/document/file.php/UNI390/READING%20MATERIAL/The%20operational%20code%20approach%20to%20profiling%20political%20leaders%20understanding%20Vladimir%20Putin.pdf))

**Input requirements.** Any verbal record attributable to the subject: speeches, interviews, press conferences, memoranda. Walker/Schafer/Young's VICS operationalization was explicitly built to run on public material "at a distance" — no interview or cooperation from the subject required. Larger corpora produce more stable index scores; a 2026 *Political Psychology* paper by Young formalizes this into precision-based minimum word counts rather than the older rule-of-thumb thresholds. ([Wiley — Young 2026](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/pops.70087))

**Coding procedure (VICS, concrete).** 1) Extract every verb-phrase in which the subject is the grammatical or logical subject describing self, or describing an "other" (opponent/ally). 2) Edit each into subject–verb–object form. 3) Classify the verb along two axes: *direction* (positive/cooperative vs. negative/conflictual — words like "support," "praise," "help" vs. "attack," "oppose," "threaten") and *intensity* (a 1–3 scale from words/gestures up through military force, per the World Event/Interaction Survey verb category scheme VICS inherits from). 4) Aggregate into indices: the **P-index** (direction of the subject's own tactics — the ratio of cooperative to conflictual attributed actions) and the **I-index** (intensity of those tactics). Similar indices are computed for beliefs about "others'" behavior, yielding the philosophical belief about whether the political universe is friendly. Walker et al. (1998) defined seventeen such indices in total covering all ten questions quantitatively. Software: **Profiler Plus**, a Common Lisp NLP platform built by Social Science Automation (Michael D. Young), used to automate VICS, LTA, and related codings at scale. ([ResearchGate — VICS](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/304754645_Operational_Code_Analysis_at_a_Distance_The_Verbs_in_Context_system_of_Content_Analysis), [VICS hand-coding manual](https://www.scribd.com/document/97851505/2008-KU-Workshop-VICS-Hand-Coding-Revised-Manual-Dr-Stephen-Walker), [Social Science Automation](http://socialscience.net/tech/ProfilerPlus.aspx), [profilerplus.org](https://profilerplus.org/))

**Validity evidence.** Content/construct/face validity is reasonably well supported — VICS scores discriminate known leaders in theoretically sensible directions (e.g., Khrushchev's operational code shifts around the Cuban Missile Crisis in the predicted direction). Renshon (2009) directly tested the public-vs-private-material criticism by comparing operational codes derived from public speeches against codes derived from private/declassified material for the same leaders and found the two converge — "tentative confirmation" that public material is usable as a proxy. But Schafer's own review concedes operational codes "may not add to the ability to either predict or explain consequential decisions." Solid construct validity, weak-to-absent demonstrated predictive validity against actual crisis decisions. ([Renshon 2009 PDF](https://jonathan-renshon.squarespace.com/s/Renshon-When-Public-Statements-Reveal-Private-Beliefs-copy.pdf), [Wiley abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-9221.2009.00718.x))

**Predictive or descriptive?** Descriptive/diagnostic, with contested and modest predictive claims about crisis behavior and risk orientation (via linkage to prospect theory). Treat as a structured belief-system snapshot, not a forecasting tool.

**Implementable as.** An LLM pipeline can (a) extract every subject-verb-object clause attributed to the target across the corpus, (b) classify direction and intensity per clause using a rubric prompt calibrated against the VICS manual's example set, (c) compute the same index arithmetic Profiler Plus does. What breaks: pronoun resolution and clause extraction on informal/spoken transcripts is noisy; LLMs will over-attribute intensity to rhetorical flourishes vs. literal policy commitments; separating "self" statements from reported/quoted others' statements requires discourse-level attribution the model can silently get wrong. This is the most mechanically well-specified method on the list and the easiest to get an LLM to approximate faithfully, precisely because it reduces to a verb-classification task.

## 2. Leadership Trait Analysis (Margaret Hermann)

**What it measures.** Seven traits derived from *linguistic proxies*, not self-report: belief in ability to control events (BACE), need for power (PWR), self-confidence (SC), conceptual complexity (CC), distrust of others (DIS), in-group bias (IGB), and task vs. relationship focus. Combinations yield eight leadership "types" (expansionistic, evangelistic, incremental, etc.) used heavily in the CIA/State Department at-a-distance tradition. ([Wooster thesis](https://openworks.wooster.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=11628&context=independentstudy))

**Input requirements.** Hermann's rule: prefer **spontaneous** material — press-conference answers, interview responses — over prepared speeches, because staff/speechwriters contaminate prepared text. Her published threshold is **50 interview responses of 100+ words each** (roughly 5,000 words minimum) per leader for a reliable profile. ([e-ir.info — Assad LTA](https://www.e-ir.info/pdf/98355))

**Coding procedure.** Word/phrase counting against trait-specific dictionaries run through Profiler Plus, normalized as a percentage of total codable verbs/words, then each trait classified low/average/high relative to a **norming group** — critically, LTA scores are always relative, not absolute. Separate norming distributions exist for U.S. presidents, world leaders generally, and a norm group of women in national politics specifically. Exact published means/SDs live in Hermann's coding scheme documentation and ProfilerPlus/PsyCL dataset materials rather than any single searchable paper. ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/253070340_Assessing_Leadership_Style_A_Trait_Analysis), [PsyCL FAQ](https://www.psycldataset.com/faq/))

**Validity evidence.** Widely used in peer-reviewed political psychology (dozens of single-leader case studies: Ardern, Assad, Putin, Erdoğan). A 2024 *Foreign Policy Analysis* paper found LTA scores shift meaningfully by *topic domain* — evidence the measure picks up something real and context-sensitive, but also a threat to the idea of a single stable trait score. ([Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/fpa/article-abstract/22/3/orag022/8728445))

**Predictive or descriptive?** Primarily descriptive/typological; downstream links from low complexity + high distrust to escalation-prone crisis behavior are correlational case-study evidence, not tested forecasting.

**Implementable as.** An LLM can approximate the dictionary-count logic more cheaply than a semantic re-derivation, since the original method is essentially a lexicon count normalized by codable-verb count. What breaks: sourcing enough *bona fide spontaneous* Q&A material (modern public figures over-rely on scripted media; podcasts blur the spontaneous/prepared line Hermann's 1980s rules didn't anticipate); and building your own norming distribution, since Hermann's original norms are proprietary/hard to obtain, meaning low/average/high classification needs a self-constructed comparison set rather than her validated one. This materially weakens interpretability claims.

## 3. Integrative Complexity (Suedfeld & Tetlock)

**What it measures.** Structural complexity of reasoning about a topic — not *what* someone believes but *how many dimensions* they consider and whether they integrate those dimensions into higher-order tradeoffs, independent of ideology or intelligence.

**Input requirements.** Any paragraph-level text where the subject reasons about an issue. Scored per paragraph, then averaged; policy-relevant applications typically use dozens of paragraphs per time period for a stable score.

**Coding procedure (Baker-Brown et al. 1992 manual).** Score each paragraph 1–7: **1** = no differentiation (single "correct" perspective, no acknowledgment of tradeoffs or alternative views); **3** = full differentiation (multiple legitimate perspectives acknowledged) but no integration between them; **5** = full integration (differentiated perspectives related via an explicit higher-order principle or tradeoff logic); **7** = integration operating at more than one level. Trained coders work from example-anchored scoring manuals, blind to source/political valence. Reported inter-coder agreement frequently **exceeds .80**, among the highest reliability figures of any method in this dossier. ([HKS scoring guide](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/sites/default/files/about_us/leadership_administration/academic-deans-office/Scoring%20the%20Integrative%20Complexity%20of%20Student%20Responses_032620.pdf))

**Validity evidence.** One of the field's better-replicated measures. The most cited predictive finding is Suedfeld & Tetlock's result that low IC in leaders' communications preceded international crises that escalated to war versus those resolved peacefully. Tetlock himself later (2014) published a self-critical paper flagging confounds between IC and topic difficulty/rhetorical genre. AutoIC (Conway, Conway & Houck 2020) is a validated NLP scorer that replicated hand-coded findings on smoking-cessation transcripts, State of the Union addresses, and the ideology-complexity relationship — direct evidence automated IC scoring is not just noise. ([Tetlock 2014 PDF](https://sydneyscott.nfshost.com/pubs/Tetlock_et_al-2014-Political_Psychology.pdf), [JSPP — AutoIC](https://jspp.psychopen.eu/index.php/jspp/article/view/5261))

**Predictive or descriptive?** The strongest predictive claim in this dossier for a *specific, falsifiable* event class, though contested on case-selection grounds.

**Implementable as.** Second-best fit for LLM automation after operational code, precisely because AutoIC already proved NLP-based IC scoring works. Build a scoring prompt anchored on the 1–7 manual definitions with few-shot examples from the public manual; score every substantive paragraph; track IC trajectory over time per topic. What breaks: LLMs conflate *complex vocabulary/sentence structure* with *integrative complexity* — a paragraph can be syntactically complex and IC-1 (dogmatic), or plain-spoken and IC-5. Anchoring examples must make this explicit or the model systematically scores verbosity as complexity.

## 4. Motive Imagery / Thematic Content Analysis (David Winter)

**What it measures.** Implicit motives — need for Power (nPower), Achievement (nAch), and Affiliation/Intimacy (nAff/Int) — coded from imagery in running text, adapted from Thematic Apperception Test picture-story coding into a text-only system usable on any naturally occurring verbal material.

**Input requirements.** Continuous prose: speeches, letters, diaries, inaugural addresses. Winter has coded every U.S. president's first inaugural address as a standard comparison corpus; stable per-1,000-word density scores require several hundred words minimum per scoring unit.

**Coding procedure.** Coders identify imagery categories per sentence or clause: power imagery = one actor having impact/control/influence on another, including forceful actions, attempts to persuade, giving unsolicited help/advice, or concern for reputation; achievement imagery = competing against a standard of excellence, unique accomplishment, or long-term goal pursuit; affiliation imagery = concern for establishing/maintaining/restoring positive relationships, expressed warmth, or distress at separation. Scores normalized per 1,000 words. The manual has demonstrated interscorer and temporal reliability and converges with the original TAT-based scoring. ([Deep Blue manual](https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/handle/2027.42/117563))

**Validity evidence.** The most cited *specific* predictive claims: Winter's presidential motive profiles correlate with independent greatness ratings, and power motivation in leaders' communications is associated with subsequent war involvement, with an interaction of power motivation and "responsibility" predicting pro-social vs. destructive use of power (Winter & Barenbaum 1985). Directly contested: a critical review (*Inference*, "Prophesying War") argues Winter's crisis case-matching is not apples-to-apples — many "matched" peaceful-vs-war crisis pairs are drawn from the same conflict at different phases, and some "peacefully resolved" cases (Munich 1938, the Compromise of 1850) are historically regarded as failures or preludes to war, which undercuts the causal claim. ([Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/the-personality-analyst/200810/presidential-personality-part-3-power-motivation), [Inference Review critique](https://inference-review.com/article/prophesying-war))

**Predictive or descriptive?** Marketed and frequently cited as predictive; the underlying case-selection methodology for the strongest claims has been credibly challenged. Treat as suggestive correlational pattern, not validated forecast.

**Implementable as.** Straightforward LLM task — clause-level classification into power/achievement/affiliation categories per Winter's manual definitions, normalized per 1,000 words, tracked over time. What breaks: the power/achievement distinction is subtler than it looks ("I will fight for X" can be power imagery, achievement imagery, or neither depending on whether it's about impact-on-others vs. meeting-a-standard) and requires anchored few-shot calibration, or the LLM over-codes assertive language generically as "power."

## 5. Psychobiography and the Profiling Tradition (Langer, Post)

**What it measures.** Not a coding scheme but a genre: deep single-subject case studies combining biography, psychodynamic or trait theory, and document analysis into a holistic personality account.

**Walter Langer / OSS Hitler study (1943).** Commissioned by the OSS using interviews with people who knew Hitler, captured documents, and published material — never a direct examination. It correctly forecast suicide as the most likely end-state and flagged the possibility of an internal coup. But the report is now judged deeply flawed: heavy reliance on secondhand rumor and hearsay, a specific widely-doubted claim (that Hitler witnessed parental intercourse as formative trauma) built on thin sourcing, and a manuscript edited/revised multiple times between drafts and publication, complicating any assessment of what was actually predicted in advance versus adjusted after. The founding case study for the field and also the founding cautionary tale about sourcing quality. ([CIA museum](https://www.cia.gov/legacy/museum/artifact/a-psychological-analysis-of-adolph-hitler-by-walter-langer), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Charles_Langer))

**Jerrold Post / CIA Center for the Analysis of Personality and Political Behavior.** Post built and ran the CIA's dedicated at-a-distance profiling unit for 21 years, producing leader profiles (notably the Camp David profiles of Begin and Sadat for Carter, later published profiles of Saddam Hussein and Bill Clinton) used directly in summit-preparation and crisis contexts. The one tradition here with documented operational use in real high-stakes decisions. What made Post's work more credible than Langer's: a standing institutional apparatus with career analysts, systematic source triangulation across biography/associates/public record, explicit purpose-limitation (briefing negotiators, not diagnosing for public consumption), and iterative refinement against real-world outcomes over decades rather than a single wartime rush job. ([GW Today](https://gwtoday.gwu.edu/memoriam-jerrold-m-post), [UMich Press](https://press.umich.edu/Books/T/The-Psychological-Assessment-of-Political-Leaders2))

**Validity evidence.** Neither tradition offers systematic replicable validity statistics. Qualitative holistic case work, evaluated case-by-case on whether specific predictions panned out. The anonymized-case tradition in clinical/forensic profiling has the same structural problem: hit rate is almost never formally tracked across a large enough sample to compute a real accuracy statistic.

**Predictive or descriptive?** Almost entirely descriptive/narrative, occasionally yielding specific falsifiable predictions that succeed or fail individually rather than as a validated method.

**Implementable as.** Not a method to operationalize directly — it's the *genre the final output belongs to*. The operational lesson from Post vs. Langer: source triangulation, explicit per-claim citation, and purpose-scoping (what decision is this profile meant to inform) are what separate credible profiling from Barnum-effect narrative-building. Bake Post's discipline into citation requirements rather than trying to code psychobiography as a scoring scheme.

## 6. Personality from Text — Computational (LIWC, Yarkoni, WWBP/Schwartz)

**What it measures.** Big Five (sometimes HEXACO) trait estimates from word-frequency patterns, via closed-vocabulary dictionaries (LIWC) or data-driven open-vocabulary machine learning (Yarkoni; Schwartz's World Well-Being Project).

**Input requirements.** LIWC needs no minimum in principle but is noisy under a few hundred words. Open-vocabulary studies used massive corpora (Yarkoni: 694 bloggers, 115,000+ words each; Schwartz et al. 2013: 700 million words across 75,000 Facebook users) — population-scale training corpora, not per-individual scoring requirements, but they set the ceiling on achievable per-individual accuracy.

**Coding procedure.** LIWC: count words in ~80 predefined psycholinguistic categories, normalize by total word count, correlate category percentages against trait scores. Open-vocabulary: no predefined categories — extract all words/phrases/topics (LDA-style clusters), fit supervised regression against ground-truth trait scores from a large labeled population, apply fitted weights to new text.

**Validity evidence — the actual numbers.** The 2023 meta-analysis "The Kernel of Truth in Text-Based Personality Assessment" pooled 31 samples (n=85,724) of LIWC-vs-Big-Five correlations: **self-reported** personality correlates with LIWC categories at only **|ρ| = .08–.14** for the strongest categories, with the 52 LIWC categories explaining on average just **5.1% of variance** in self-reported personality. Against **observer-reported** personality, correlations were meaningfully higher at **|ρ| = .18–.39**, explaining **38.5% of variance** — real but far from diagnostic. ([Tilburg repository](https://repository.tilburguniversity.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/6954e6ad-d510-43fb-bda3-54d102be8eb3/content)) Yarkoni's 2010 blog study found many statistically significant but individually small single-word correlations — real signal, diffuse and non-obvious rather than "one big tell." ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0092656610000541)) Schwartz et al.'s open-vocabulary model hits **91.9% accuracy for binary gender** (an easier target), while well-being prediction from text tops out around the meta-analytic **r = .33** ceiling. ([PLOS ONE 2013](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0073791))

**Predictive or descriptive?** Correlational/descriptive at the population level; at the individual level these correlations are far too weak to support a confident claim about any one person's trait score from text alone.

**Implementable as.** Cheapest method to run. But: if you have an LLM eyeball a corpus and output Big Five scores, you are not running LIWC or WWBP's method at all — you are asking the model to pattern-match "sounds like a [trait]" impressions, a fundamentally different and much less validated operation that reintroduces exactly the Barnum/stereotype risk this dossier warns about. To claim you implement this literature honestly, either (a) replicate LIWC's actual dictionary-count-then-correlate procedure and report the resulting weak correlation honestly, or (b) don't claim Big Five scores from this pipeline at all.

## 7. Personal Construct Theory / Repertory Grid (George Kelly)

**What it measures.** Not a fixed trait framework — the person's *own* dimensions of judgment, elicited rather than imposed. The theoretical commitment is that people organize experience via bipolar constructs ("honest–dishonest," "ambitious–content") idiosyncratic to the individual; the method's value is *discovering which constructs this specific person uses* rather than scoring them on someone else's scale.

**Input requirements — the core adaptation problem.** The canonical repertory grid is a live structured interview: the researcher presents "elements" three at a time (triads) and asks "in what way are two of these alike, and different from the third?" Each answer yields one bipolar construct; the subject then rates all elements on each construct, producing a grid. This is fundamentally an *elicited, live, interactive* method — the entire point is the subject supplying novel dimensions the researcher couldn't have listed in advance. ([EduTechWiki](https://edutechwiki.unige.ch/en/Repertory_grid_technique), [ub.edu manual](https://www.ub.edu/terdep/gridcor2002/pag/man1.html))

**Coding procedure adapted for corpus-only use.** You cannot run the live triad interview on a dead or non-cooperating subject. The at-a-distance adaptation substitutes: (a) mine the corpus for every instance where the subject explicitly contrasts two people/things/policies ("X, unlike Y, actually cares about..."), (b) treat each contrast as a naturally-occurring construct elicitation and extract the implicit bipolar dimension, (c) aggregate recurring dimensions into the subject's own construct system, (d) use the corpus's implicit ratings to fill a synthetic grid. A real but nonstandard adaptation — nobody has published a validated "corpus repertory grid" methodology, so this is *inspired-by*, not *implementing*.

**Validity evidence.** Rich validity literature for the live interview method across clinical, consumer-research, and organizational contexts; essentially none for a corpus-only substitute, because that substitute doesn't exist as a studied method.

**Predictive or descriptive?** Descriptive by design — mapping the subject's own meaning-structure, not forecasting behavior.

**Implementable as.** The single most valuable non-standard contribution an LLM pipeline could make, precisely because LLMs are good at finding implicit contrast structures across a large corpus that a human coder would need weeks to surface. But be explicit in the output that this is an *adaptation inspired by PCT*, not a validated implementation — the live-elicitation requirement is not optional polish in the original, it's the mechanism that makes constructs genuinely the subject's own rather than the analyst's projection.

## 8. Values Frameworks (Schwartz circumplex, Moral Foundations Theory)

**What it measures.** Schwartz: ten (or four higher-order) basic values in a motivational circumplex where adjacent values are compatible and opposite values conflict (self-direction/stimulation vs. security/conformity). The core tension the theory resolves is *which value wins when two compete*. MFT: five (sometimes six, with Liberty) moral intuition foundations — Care/harm, Fairness/cheating, Loyalty/betrayal, Authority/subversion, Sanctity/degradation — theorized as evolved, largely automatic reactions rather than reasoned conclusions.

**Input requirements.** Schwartz's native instrument (Portrait Values Questionnaire) is self-report, not text-native. MFT has native text tools: the original Moral Foundations Dictionary (Graham & Haidt, hand-curated, 200+ words/foundation) and the improved **extended Moral Foundations Dictionary (eMFD)**, built from large-scale crowd-sourced human annotations rather than researcher intuition. ([Graham/Haidt PDF](https://cpb-us-e2.wpmucdn.com/sites.uci.edu/dist/1/863/files/2020/06/Graham-et-al-2013.AESP_.pdf), [eMFD Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13428-020-01433-0))

**Coding procedure.** Dictionary/frequency counting of foundation-associated words, normalized by document length, yielding a moral-foundation profile per text or time period. Value-conflict resolution ("which value wins") is measured by *revealed priority in contexts of stated tradeoff* — search the corpus for places the subject explicitly chooses between two competing goods and code which pole wins, since frequency alone conflates "talks about fairness a lot" with "prioritizes fairness over loyalty when they conflict."

**Validity evidence.** eMFD's inter-annotator agreement is **0.42–0.92** depending on foundation, and construct validity was triangulated against independent topic classifiers — eMFD Fairness correlates with news about Legislation at **r = .33** and Free Speech at **r = .12**; Authority with Protest coverage at **r = .24**, Rebellion at **r = .16**. Modest-to-moderate but real, and notably better documented than the original MFD. Self-report MFQ correlations with foundation-matched language are "particularly robust for Harm and Fairness," weaker for the other three. ([eMFD Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13428-020-01433-0))

**Predictive or descriptive?** Descriptive of moral/value emphasis; predicts stated political positions reasonably well but is not validated as a predictor of individual behavioral decisions.

**Implementable as.** eMFD-style dictionary scoring is directly implementable and cheap; the harder, more valuable piece — explicit tradeoff detection — is a genuine LLM strength (finding argumentative structure) that dictionary methods cannot do at all. This is a place where an LLM pipeline can exceed the published baseline rather than approximate it. What breaks: dictionary-only scoring conflates topic (talking *about* fairness because the news cycle is about fairness) with value (actually prioritizing fairness) unless gated on the tradeoff-detection layer.

## 9. Other Genuinely Relevant Methods

- **Cognitive mapping (Axelrod, 1976).** Graphs a decision-maker's stated causal beliefs ("X leads to Y") as nodes and directed edges from text. Directly implementable: an LLM can extract causal-claim clauses across a corpus, build a cause-map graph per subject, then diff two subjects' maps of the same topic to locate where disagreement is about *values* versus about *causal beliefs*. ([Structure of Decision](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/11406150-structure-of-decision))
- **Image theory (Boulding 1959; Herrmann 1986/1997/2013).** Codes a leader's schema of another actor (ally/enemy/dependent/degenerate images) from perceived goal compatibility, relative power, and relative cultural status — useful for profiling how a subject perceives *other specific actors*, not just general disposition. Herrmann's experimental work found these images function as predictive independent variables for policy choice. ([Herrmann PDF](http://josephciarrochi.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/herman-voss-et-al-ciarrochi-images-in-international-relations-an-experiemntal-test-of-cognitive-schema.pdf))
- **Narrative identity (McAdams).** Codes autobiographical narrative for agency, communion, redemption sequences (bad-to-good arcs) and contamination sequences (good-to-bad arcs), coherence, and complexity. Redemption-heavy narrators score higher on generativity and well-being. Requires *first-person autobiographical narrative* specifically, which most public figures' corpora contain only sparsely — a real corpus-availability constraint. ([Sage — McAdams 2001](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0146167201274008))
- **Regulatory focus / construal level from text.** LIWC-style dictionaries exist and are validated cross-lingually (promotion: achieve, ideal; prevention: afraid, fail), theoretically linked to construal level. Cheap, modest validity, same dictionary limitations. ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10358899/))
- **Tetlock's Good Judgment Project.** Not a profiling method — it's about *forecasting accuracy of the analyst*. Relevant only insofar as system outputs that forecast a subject's likely future decisions should be evaluated the way GJP evaluates forecasters: calibration and Brier scores against resolved outcomes, not "sounds right." Superforecasters beat professional intelligence analysts by roughly 30%, driven by IQ, political knowledge, and active open-minded thinking — not by any special profiling technique. ([goodjudgment.com](https://goodjudgment.com/about/))

## Validity and Failure Modes

**Which methods have real predictive validity, ranked.**
1. **Integrative complexity** — best-documented specific predictive claim (IC decline preceding war), highest inter-coder reliability (>.80), independent NLP replication via AutoIC. Tetlock's own 2014 self-critique flags topic-difficulty confounds.
2. **Motive imagery (Winter)** — most quoted predictive headline (power motivation → war), but the case-matching methodology has a specific credible published critique that meaningfully weakens the causal claim.
3. **Operational code** — good construct validity (Renshon's public-vs-private convergence), but leading practitioners concede it adds little to actual behavior prediction.
4. **LTA and computational personality-from-text** — essentially descriptive/classificatory; LIWC's meta-analytic ceiling (5.1% of self-reported variance) makes any strong individual-level claim from word frequency indefensible.
5. **Psychobiography, PCT-as-adapted, values frameworks, narrative identity, image theory, cognitive mapping** — descriptive tools with case-level or modest correlational support, not validated forecasting instruments.

**Inter-coder reliability, actually reported.** IC: >.80 is standard. eMFD: 0.42–0.92 by foundation. VICS and LTA reliability figures are less consistently reported — most papers state that trained coders reached acceptable agreement without publishing the coefficient, itself a documentation gap worth noting if benchmarking an LLM against a human baseline that isn't well quantified in the first place.

**The at-a-distance validity debate.** The central fight is public-vs-private material. Renshon's empirical test (public and private material yield convergent operational codes) is the strongest evidence for using public corpora; his own caveat stands, that private material isn't automatically truer either, since leaders strategize in front of advisors too. Speechwriter/ghostwriter contamination is why Hermann built spontaneous-material rules into LTA from the start; it remains unresolved for modern public figures with communications staff, ghostwritten books, and partly staff-run social accounts. **A system needs source-type metadata (spontaneous interview vs. drafted speech vs. ghostwritten book vs. staff-run social account) as a first-class field, not an afterthought**, because several methods' validity claims are conditioned on getting that distinction right. Translation effects apply to any non-English-primary subject — VICS and IC coding are sensitive to verb/clause structure that doesn't survive translation cleanly. Selection bias in what gets published and preserved is structural and unfixable by better coding: the corpus is whatever survived and got digitized, skewed toward formal, public, filtered material. ([Walker 2000 symposium](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/0162-895X.00207))

**Barnum/Forer effect, ecological fallacy, fundamental attribution error.** Forer's 1949 demonstration is the load-bearing cautionary result: subjects rated a **generic, vague personality description pulled from an astrology book** as highly accurate for themselves personally, when every subject received identical text. Any profile output must pass a specific test: is this claim specific enough to be *falsified* by the corpus, or is it a Barnum statement ("values loyalty but is also independent-minded") that sounds true of nearly anyone? The ecological fallacy risk is method-specific: LTA and values-from-text norms are built from *group* distributions, and applying a group-level frequency norm to conclude something about *this individual's* psychology is exactly the inferential move the fallacy warns against. Fundamental attribution error shows up as the temptation to read every strong or hostile statement as revealing a stable trait rather than a response to a specific situational pressure the corpus also records — a good pipeline surfaces situational context alongside any trait-language claim rather than stripping it out. ([Barnum effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnum_effect), [Dickson & Kelly 1985](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.2466/pr0.1985.57.2.367))

**The Goldwater Rule.** APA ethics rule (1973, tightened 2017) bars psychiatrists from offering a professional opinion on a public figure's mental state without direct examination and consent. Its one explicit carve-out is "psychological profiling of historical figures aimed at enhancing public and governmental understanding of their conditions," provided it stops short of clinical diagnosis. This binds licensed psychiatrists, not software, and doesn't bind LTA/VICS/IC-style political psychology at all. But it's the right frame to borrow: score belief structures, traits-as-linguistic-patterns, and value emphases — never output clinical/diagnostic language about any real, named, living person. That line separates "operational code analysis of a public figure" (established, defensible) from "unlicensed diagnosis of a real person" (real reputational and possibly legal exposure). ([psychiatry.org](https://www.psychiatry.org/news-room/goldwater-rule), [JAAPL](https://jaapl.org/content/44/2/226))

**Base rates.** A profile that "sounds right" is nearly always worthless as evidence, because base rates for most trait/style descriptors are high enough that almost any specific claim ("ambitious," "distrustful of outsiders," "values loyalty") matches most people most of the time. A profiling claim earns evidential weight only if it is *specific and falsifiable against the corpus* (a directly quotable, sourced instance) and *discriminating* (would this claim be false for a plausible comparison set of similar public figures, or is it true of essentially anyone in that role). Build the discrimination test into the system: for every claim, require the pipeline to also state what evidence would have falsified it and whether comparable figures score differently.

**What happens when an LLM does the coding instead of a trained human.** The empirical literature is genuinely mixed. Recent studies (2025) find LLM inter-rater reliability with human coders reaching *substantial* agreement (κ>0.6) on some qualitative-coding tasks after prompt optimization, and comparable-to-human performance on latent content analysis. But reliability tracks task difficulty closely: where human coders themselves disagree (sarcasm detection), LLM agreement with humans is correspondingly poor, and LLM-human agreement correlates with human-human agreement across tasks — LLMs are not a fix for an underlying ambiguous construct, they inherit its ambiguity. ([arXiv 2508.14764](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.14764v1), [Nature Sci Rep 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-96508-3))

**The failure mode specific to profiling a named, famous, real person is sharper than generic annotation studies capture.** An LLM coding a transcript of a well-known public figure has almost certainly seen extensive commentary, caricature, and received wisdom about that person during training, and the live coding task competes against a pre-existing stereotype the model may pattern-match to instead of the actual text in front of it — scoring a passage as "high need for power" not because the verb imagery in that passage supports it but because the model already "knows" that figure is described that way. Guardrails:

1. Force every coded judgment to cite the specific span of text that produced it, so stereotype-matching without textual support is visible and auditable.
2. Where feasible, run the coding prompt on excerpts with the speaker's identity masked/anonymized and compare against the identified-speaker version. **A large gap between the two is a direct measurement of stereotype contamination.**
3. Treat any claim the model cannot tie to a specific quotable passage as unsupported by construction, not merely lower-confidence.

## If You Were Building This System

**Implement first, in this order:**

1. **Operational Code / VICS-style verb-clause coding.** Most mechanically well-specified method here — reduces to clause extraction plus two-axis verb classification, close to what LLMs already do well. Gives a belief-system snapshot (view of the competitive world, preferred tactics) with the best-documented public-material validity test in the literature (Renshon's convergence result) behind it.

2. **Integrative Complexity scoring.** Best inter-rater reliability of any method here, an already-validated automated version (AutoIC) to benchmark against, and the most specific predictive claim in the literature to make outputs falsifiable rather than vibes-based. Also yields a trajectory metric (complexity over time and topic) that is genuinely interesting and hard to fake.

3. **Values/moral-foundations tradeoff detection.** Cheap eMFD dictionary layer for a baseline moral-emphasis profile, plus the genuinely LLM-advantaged layer of finding explicit value-conflict-resolution moments in the corpus. This is where the pipeline can produce something the published dictionary methods structurally cannot, and it directly answers the question users actually ask.

4. **Motive imagery (Winter-style), with the war/peace predictive claim explicitly excluded.** Worth implementing for the trait-density profile and the existing leader benchmark corpus, but present as descriptive motive density, not as a validated predictor of conflict behavior, and say so in the output.

**Skip or deprioritize:**

- **LTA as originally specified.** Not because the seven traits are uninteresting, but because you don't have Hermann's proprietary norming distributions, so you cannot honestly claim "high/average/low relative to world leaders." You'd be inventing your own norm group, quietly breaking the comparability claim that makes LTA useful in the first place.
- **Full computational Big Five as a headline feature.** The meta-analytic ceiling (5.1% of self-reported variance) is low enough that presenting a "Big Five score" as a primary output over-claims what the literature supports.
- **Repertory grid / PCT as literally specified.** The live-elicitation requirement is the mechanism that makes it valid; anything corpus-only is an invention inspired by PCT, so don't market it with PCT's validity claims attached.
- **Psychobiography as a module.** It's a genre and a discipline, not a coding scheme. Absorb its lesson into citation and sourcing requirements system-wide.
- **Good Judgment Project technique.** Not a profiling method. Borrow its evaluation discipline (calibration tracking against resolved outcomes), not its elicitation technique.

**The one structural requirement that cuts across everything.** Every method here with real validity evidence earned it through *anchored, example-calibrated coding against a public manual* (IC's Baker-Brown manual, VICS's hand-coding manual, Winter's scoring manual) plus *reported reliability figures*. If the pipeline skips the anchoring and just asks a model to "assess integrative complexity" from its general knowledge of what the term means, it is not implementing the validated method — it is an uncalibrated approximation, and the entire validity section above stops transferring to the output. **The anchoring step is not optional polish. It is the difference between "implements Suedfeld & Tetlock's method" and "sounds like it does."**
